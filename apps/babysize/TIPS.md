# Everyday tips and their evidence

Original panel copy by [cptntrps](https://github.com/cptntrps), developed with OpenAI
Codex. Guidance checked on **2026-10-10**. Sources are NHS and NIH; those
organizations have not reviewed or endorsed this app.

A short **TRY THIS** card follows the playful milestone. The suggestions focus on
everyday habits: varied meals, hydration, rest, comfortable walking, winding down,
sharing chores, talking to the baby, and bringing questions to prenatal visits.

These are general pregnancy suggestions. Individual medical advice takes priority.
The water card explains that dehydration can cause headaches; it does not assume
that a headache is caused by dehydration or suggest delaying medical care. The
sources below include when symptoms need professional attention.

Week placement is editorial, not a schedule for starting a habit or a claim that
it triggers the milestone. Voice cards begin at week 21. Most suggestions apply
throughout pregnancy; the small-meals suggestion is shown in early pregnancy.
The app does not replace prenatal care or supplement guidance.

The 15-second cycle is week (3s), size (3s), playful development (4s), then tip (5s).
The tip header credits its main source. Before week 4 and from week 41 onward,
the tip is a prenatal-care prompt.

## variety

**English:** VARY THE MENU / FOR A MIX / OF NUTRIENTS

**Portuguese:** VARIE O PRATO / PARA COMBINAR / NUTRIENTES

A varied, balanced diet helps supply the nutrients needed during pregnancy. Variety matters across meals and days; no particular food needs to be eaten for a weekly milestone.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/have-a-healthy-diet/).

## water

**English:** SIP WATER / DEHYDRATION CAN / CAUSE HEADACHES

**Portuguese:** POUCA AGUA / PODE CAUSAR / DOR DE CABECA

Drinking fluids helps prevent dehydration, which can contribute to headaches. Water is not a treatment for every headache. A severe headache, vision changes, pain under the ribs, or sudden swelling in pregnancy needs prompt maternity or medical advice; do not delay care to try hydration.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/headaches/), [source 2](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/headaches/).

## rest

**English:** FEELING TIRED? / TIME TO PUT / YOUR FEET UP

**Portuguese:** BATEU CANSACO? / TIRE UM TEMPO / PARA DESCANSAR

Tiredness is common in pregnancy. Making time for rest and adequate sleep can make everyday life more manageable.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/tiredness/).

## walk

**English:** A SHORT WALK / AT YOUR OWN / EASY PACE

**Portuguese:** UMA CAMINHADA / CURTA NO SEU / PROPRIO RITMO

Gentle activity such as walking can support fitness, sleep, and mood. Start gradually, stay comfortable, and follow any activity restrictions from your maternity team. Stop if you feel unwell or uncomfortable.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/keeping-well/exercise/).

## meals

**English:** FEELING SICK? / SMALLER MEALS / MAY HELP

**Portuguese:** COM ENJOO? / PORCOES MENORES / MAIS VEZES

Small, frequent meals may help with pregnancy nausea. Persistent or severe vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, or signs of dehydration need medical advice.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/vomiting-and-morning-sickness/).

## wind_down

**English:** BEFORE BED / MAKE TIME TO / WIND DOWN

**Portuguese:** ANTES DE DORMIR / TIRE UM TEMPO / PARA RELAXAR

Taking time to relax before bed may help with sleep. It is a practical comfort suggestion, not a guarantee of better sleep or fetal development.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/tiredness/).

## support

**English:** SHARE CHORES / MAKE A LITTLE / ROOM FOR REST

**Portuguese:** DIVIDA TAREFAS / ABRA ESPACO / PARA DESCANSAR

Accepting help from family or others can create time for rest. Sharing chores is a practical way for a partner or support person to help.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/pregnancy/common-symptoms/tiredness/).

## voice

**English:** CHAT OR SING / BABY CAN HEAR / YOUR VOICE

**Portuguese:** CONVERSE, CANTE / BEBE JA OUVE / SUA VOZ

The NHS describes hearing outside voices around week 21 and recognizing voices around week 31, and suggests talking or singing. This is a connection activity without a claim of improved intelligence. Normal conversation needs no belly headphones or loud sound.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/2nd-trimester/week-21/), [source 2](https://www.nhs.uk/best-start-in-life/pregnancy/week-by-week-guide-to-pregnancy/3rd-trimester/week-31/).

## prenatal

**English:** BRING QUESTIONS / TO YOUR NEXT / PRENATAL VISIT

**Portuguese:** LEVE DUVIDAS / PARA A PROXIMA / CONSULTA

Prenatal visits include time to discuss pregnancy questions and tailor care. Keeping questions ready can help you make use of that conversation.

Sources: [source 1](https://www.nichd.nih.gov/health/topics/pregnancy/conditioninfo/prenatal-care).
